An actor's headshot has a different job from a corporate one. A corporate headshot says "trust me". An actor's headshot says "this is exactly who will walk into your audition room", and it needs to make a casting director want to call you in. Here is what to focus on.

## The most important rule: look like you

Casting directors use your headshot to decide whether you fit a role. If you arrive looking noticeably different, through heavy retouching, a different hairstyle or an old photo, you waste their time and yours. Your headshot should look like you on a good day, today.

That means:

- **Natural retouching only.** Temporary blemishes can go; freckles, lines and scars that are part of your face should stay.
- **Current hair and look.** If you change your hair significantly, update your headshot.
- **Your real age range.** Do not try to look years younger than you can play.

## Shoot more than one look

Most actors benefit from two or three looks in one session:

1. **Commercial:** bright, warm and approachable, with a genuine smile. For ads, brands and friendly roles.
2. **Theatrical or dramatic:** a more serious, layered expression with more contrast. For film, web series and drama.
3. **Character (optional):** a look that matches the roles you are most often called for.

Change your top, adjust your hair, and the same session gives you options for different submissions.

## Framing and background

- **Head and shoulders** is standard, with your eyes as the sharpest point of the image.
- **Simple backgrounds**, such as grey, white or softly blurred, keep attention on your face.
- **Eye contact** with the lens matters most. Your eyes should feel alive and connected.
