For doctors, lawyers, chartered accountants and consultants, clients are not buying a product. They are choosing a person to trust with their health, their legal matters or their business. Before that first appointment, most people look you up online, and your photo is part of the decision. Here is how to make sure it works in your favour.

## Why trust professionals need a great headshot

- **Patients and clients choose a person.** Clinic websites, hospital directories, Google listings and practice profiles all show your photo next to your name.
- **First impressions are fast.** A clear, warm, professional photo signals competence and care. A blurry crop from a group photo signals the opposite.
- **Consistency builds credibility.** The same photo across your website, LinkedIn, Practo-style listings and conference profiles makes you instantly recognisable.

## Choosing the right style

### Doctors and healthcare professionals

Aim for **approachable and reassuring**. A warm, genuine smile works well. A white coat or scrubs can help patients recognise your role, but a smart shirt or blouse is also common for clinic websites. Bright, even lighting and a clean background keep the feel calm and clinical without being cold.

### Lawyers and chartered accountants

Aim for **confident and composed**. A more measured expression, formal attire and a classic grey or dark background suit legal and financial work. Many firms prefer a consistent look for every partner and associate on their website.

### Consultants and advisors

Aim for **credible and personable**. Smart business or smart casual, with a confident, relaxed smile. If you are building a personal brand, as speakers, coaches and independent consultants often are, consider a fuller [personal branding shoot](/Professional-branding-portraits) with several looks and settings.

## What to wear

- **Solid, mid-tone colours** such as navy, charcoal, deep green and soft blue
- **Well-fitted, pressed clothes.** Creases show more in a close crop
- **Simple accessories.** Avoid anything that pulls attention from your face
- **Your usual glasses**, if you wear them. Patients and clients should recognise you
