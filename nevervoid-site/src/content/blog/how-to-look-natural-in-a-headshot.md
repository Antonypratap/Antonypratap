"I'm not photogenic" is one of the most common things we hear before a headshot session. Here is the good news: almost nobody is naturally relaxed in front of a camera. Looking natural is a skill, and most of it is the photographer's job, not yours. These are the techniques we use with every client, and the small things you can do to help.

## Why most people look stiff in photos

When you know a camera is pointed at you, your body tenses: shoulders rise, the chin pulls back and the smile freezes. A phone selfie or a rushed office photo catches exactly that moment. A good headshot session is designed to get you past it.

## Before the session

- **Sleep well the night before.** Rest shows in your eyes and skin more than any edit can fix.
- **Plan your outfits in advance.** Knowing you look right removes a big source of nerves. See [what to wear for a professional headshot](/blog/what-to-wear-for-a-professional-headshot).
- **Look at a few headshots you like.** Bring two or three examples. It helps us understand the feel you want, from warm and approachable to serious and authoritative.
- **Arrive a few minutes early.** Rushing in raises your heart rate, and that shows.

## During the session

### Let the first few minutes be a warm-up

The first frames are rarely the best, and that is fine. We start talking, adjusting the light and taking test shots. Within a few minutes, most people stop noticing the camera.

### Think of a person, not a lens

A smile aimed at a lens looks empty. A smile for someone you like looks real. We will often ask you to think of a specific person or moment, and the difference shows immediately.

### Use the "almost smile"

For many professional headshots, the strongest expression is not a big grin but a relaxed, closed-mouth smile with engaged eyes. Photographers sometimes call this "squinching": a very slight narrowing of the lower eyelids that reads as confidence.

### Small posture tricks that make a big difference

- **Chin slightly forward and down.** It defines the jawline and avoids a double chin.
- **Shoulders down and relaxed.** Take a breath out just before the shot.
- **Turn your body slightly away** from the camera, then turn your face back toward it.
- **Lean in a little.** It signals engagement.

You do not need to remember any of this. We guide every pose and expression during the session, and you can see the photos as we shoot.

## Seeing the photos as you go

Reviewing images during the session helps you relax, because you can see what is working. Our [studio headshot sessions](/Professional-studio-headshots) include all photos from the session, and your best 5 are advanced-edited, so there is no pressure to get one perfect frame.

## A note on retouching

Good retouching keeps you looking like yourself: it softens temporary blemishes, stray hairs and creased clothing, but it does not change your face. People should recognise you when they meet you.
