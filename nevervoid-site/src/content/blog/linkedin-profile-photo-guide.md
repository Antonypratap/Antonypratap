Recruiters, clients and colleagues decide whether to read your profile in a second or two, and your photo is a large part of that first impression. A LinkedIn photo has a different job from a portrait on a wall. It has to work as a tiny circle on a phone screen.

## Framing for a circle

LinkedIn crops your photo into a circle and shows it at very small sizes. That means:

- **Head and top of shoulders** should fill most of the frame, with your face taking up roughly 60% of it.
- Leave a little space above your head so the circle crop doesn't cut it off.
- Look **straight into the lens**, or very slightly off it. Eye contact reads as confidence.

## Expression

The best LinkedIn photos look approachable and competent at the same time. A relaxed, closed-mouth smile or a slight open smile both work. A forced smile doesn't. ## Background

- **Plain grey or white** backgrounds are timeless and keep attention on you.
- An **environmental background**, such as your office or a clean wall in your home, can work if it is softly blurred.
- Avoid holiday photos, group crops and busy rooms.

## Lighting and quality

Soft, even light from the front flatters everyone. Phone selfies tend to distort features because the lens is so close, so a photographer working from a few metres away with a portrait lens gives a more natural face shape.

## When to update

Update your photo when your look changes noticeably (hair, glasses, beard) or every two to three years. People should recognise you when they meet you.

## Using the same photo everywhere

Consistency helps people recognise you. Use the same headshot on LinkedIn, your company page, conference profiles and email signature.
